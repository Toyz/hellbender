# 86. Three hundred things the game says

Toyz: "transport desrcrpted, and all sorts - look at how sounds and such
work". So rather than chase one line, a census.

`STARTUP.POD` ships 315 `.WAV` files. 305 of them are named somewhere in
`HELLBEND.EXE`. Of those, 101 are named by the level data - a `.NAV` point's
completion and proximity sounds, a `.DEF` type's destruction sound - which
the port already plays, because it plays whatever the file names. The other
204 are named by code, and the port names 65.

Crossing the two lists left 151 sounds the engine plays and the port never
mentions, and the list reads like a table of contents:

```
tran-des transdes tran-esc transesc tran-loc transloc   hulldamg hulllow
enlow misslock jumpzone zone-20 zone-25 zone-cls        cloakeng cloakdis
uncloak2 complete luck payback giveitup seeyou knees    ... and ninety more
```

## It is one table

[[43]] had already found a "phrase table at `0x505c20`" and copied
twenty-five lines out of it by hand as the port needed them. Nobody had
counted it.

Three hundred entries of 36 bytes: a flag, how long the subtitle stays in
16.16 seconds, and four pointers - a sound, a second sound, the subtitle,
and a fourth that is null in all three hundred.

```
 13  vul-sec.wav   + pause.wav   "Vulcan Cannon secured"
 28  objcomp.wav                 "Objective Complete"
 42  hulllow.wav                 "Hull integrity low"
 47  trp-des.wav                 "Troop transport destroyed."
 55  engcore.wav   + dest30.wav  "Engine core destroyed"
299  20-sec.wav                  "20 seconds"
```

The flag is 1 or 3 and 3 marks the ones with a second sound. That second
sound is `pause.wav` in every case but 55, which was worth checking rather
than asserting - my first test said "always" and entry 55 said otherwise.
Durations are 2, 4 or 5 seconds. Two entries at the top have a subtitle and
no sound at all.

`hb_sim::phrases` is the whole thing now, generated by reading the image, with
two tests: every entry has a sound that is a `.wav` and a duration the table
actually uses, and every sound it names is in `STARTUP.POD`.

## What this does and does not fix

It does not make the game say "Troop transport destroyed." It makes the line
*available* by its number the moment something calls for it, which is the
half that was missing: the port had twenty-five phrases and no way to reach
the other 275.

**Still unknown:** what fires each one. That is 300 triggers spread through
the image, and the method that works is the one that found the missing three
in [[66]] - take the sound, find the string, read what plays it. `trp-des`
and its neighbours are a whole family of `<thing> destroyed` lines that look
like they are keyed off a `.NAV` point or an actor class, and finding that
key would light up dozens at once.
